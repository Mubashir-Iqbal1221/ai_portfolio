Royalty payment checks for an oil and gas owner

An oil and gas royalty owner receives payment files from operators every month and could not easily tell whether it had been paid correctly. The pipeline reads those files, checks them, and flags changes from one month to the next that do not add up. I built the anomaly detection and optimised the pipeline.

At a glance

  • Client: an oil and gas royalty owner
  • My role: built the cross-month anomaly detection, moved the pipeline's working files to Parquet, and optimised the pipeline.
  • Result: odd payments flagged across months, each one explained by its rule
  • Stack: Python, pandas, Parquet, FastAPI

The problem

Operators send royalty data as fixed-width files, each in its own format. The data is large and arrives every month, and the errors that matter are easy to miss by hand: revenue that changes while volume does not, a property that quietly stops paying, or a change in the owner's share that shifts revenue.

What I built and decided

Each file is read, checked record by record, converted, and its totals cross-checked, for two operators' formats. On top of that:

  • Three kinds of anomaly, compared across months:
    • Revenue that moves without a matching change in volume, or the reverse.
    • Properties that drop to zero, disappear, or appear for the first time.
    • Revenue that changes alongside a change in the owner's share.
  • Rules, not a model. Every flag can be explained to the person checking payments, and thresholds can be adjusted without retraining anything.
  • January compared with the right month. January is checked against the previous December, so the year boundary does not break the comparison.
  • Parquet instead of CSV. The pipeline's working files moved to Parquet, read and written in chunks, so large months are processed in pieces.
