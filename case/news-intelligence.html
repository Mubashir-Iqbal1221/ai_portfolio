News intelligence for a telecom group

A telecom group wanted early warning from the news on regulation, competitors, prices and payments, so its leaders could act before their competitors. I designed the pipeline that turns a stream of news into short, scored alerts, each linked to its sources.

At a glance

  • Client: a telecom group in the Middle East and Latin America
  • My role: main engineer on the alert pipeline in the first version. Designed the pipeline end to end and led its engineering in the MVP.
  • Result: F1 0.88 for the relevance filter, on a 132-item benchmark I built and labelled, over half of it synthetic
  • Stack: Python, FastAPI, PostgreSQL with pgvector, Gemini, Terraform, Google Cloud Run

The problem

The product had to read a constant stream of news across several markets and three languages. It had to keep only what could affect the business and turn that into alerts a leader could check and act on.

The hardest part showed up during building: much of the news that matters never mentions telecom. In one case, rising fuel prices, higher fees for foreign workers and dearer transport together meant many prepaid customers had less money left for top-ups. None of those stories names a phone company. At the same time, stories that do name a telecom brand, like a sports sponsorship, often do not matter. The filter had to judge how a story could reach the business, not whether it mentions it.

What I built and decided

  • Stages, not one agent. The first version used a single agent for every decision. I split the MVP into stages that each do one job and save their result, so each one can be tested on its own.
  • Context as impact paths. I led the work to describe the business as the ways outside news reaches it, including what should not count. This is what lets indirect stories through.
  • The model labels and writes; code decides. Every score comes from plain functions with versioned weights, so any alert's score can be traced and reproduced.
  • Nothing published changes. Published alerts are locked, and new information arrives as a follow-up. I also fixed claim extraction, so a new article can add to a story but never rewrite it.

I built the scoring, the extraction fix and the benchmark myself, and set up the Google Cloud infrastructure with Terraform and CI/CD.

Limits and next steps

Indirect news, at 0.57, is the main open problem. The labels are mine and have not been reviewed by anyone else. The scoring weights are starting values that have not yet been checked against real outcomes. Next steps are an outside review of the labels, a dated re-run of the benchmark on the current context, and splitting the API from the background work.
